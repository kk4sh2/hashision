Hashision Lab

Inspect digests, compare them bit by bit, and watch the birthday paradox produce a collision in a deliberately shortened hash.

Scope. Collisions here are found in a truncated digest of 8–24 bits. The complete MD5, SHA-1, SHA-256 and SHA-512 hashes stay different, and this page shows you both so you can check. Nothing on this page breaks a real hash function.

Type anything and see all four digests at once. The first 16 bits of each are highlighted — that is the slice the collision lab searches.

Compare two texts, two digests you paste in, or verify that a candidate text produces a digest you were given.

Paste a digest and this works out which algorithm could have produced it, from its length and character set.

Random strings are hashed and their digests cut down to a few leading bits. Each truncated value goes into a lookup table; the search stops the moment one repeats with a different input.

Change one character and watch roughly half the output bits flip. Each square is one bit of the digest; copper means that bit changed.

Part of the Hashision project — the command-line version adds file hashing, benchmarking over many runs and JSON/CSV/TXT reports. Everything here runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded.
